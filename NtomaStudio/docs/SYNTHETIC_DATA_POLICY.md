# Can we synthesise the 50,000-image dataset?

**Date:** 2026-09-24 · **Question:** can the dataset be generated rather than collected?
**Short answer: no — and generating it would make the app worse, not better.**

This is not a capability limitation being passed off as a principle. Six AI-generated
fabric images were produced and measured against real photographs for this document, in
the course of which two things became clear: the images are genuinely convincing, and
they are also unusable — for legal reasons, for technical reasons, and for one reason
that is specific to this app's taxonomy.

---

## 1. What was actually measured

Six images were generated for this evaluation (kente woven, kente print, real wax print,
tie-dye, denim), then four more kente variants from an *identical* prompt to test
diversity. They were compared against 5 real kente photographs and the 15 real catalog
photos using the app's own GLCM implementation (`TextureMetrics.glcm`, 16 levels, offsets
(1,0) and (0,1), symmetrised) plus frequency-domain and metadata forensics.

### Finding 1 — they are visually convincing

At a glance the generated kente cloth shows strip seams, geometric blocks and plausible
colour; the wax print shows crackle texture and ring motifs. A human skimming a folder
would not catch these. This is the part that makes the idea attractive.

### Finding 2 — they defeat the app's texture heuristics

This is the most important technical result, and it is the opposite of what was expected:

| metric (app's own GLCM) | REAL (n=15) | GENERATED (n=6) |
|---|---|---|
| contrast | 18.04 ± 11.23 | 25.23 ± 5.59 |
| homogeneity | 0.288 ± 0.139 | 0.208 ± 0.024 |
| energy | 0.0144 ± 0.0236 | 0.0048 ± 0.0005 |
| correlation | 0.398 ± 0.207 | 0.295 ± 0.122 |
| **noise floor** | **2.93 ± 2.33** | **9.51 ± 4.81** |

**Generated images scored *higher* on the noise metric than real photographs.** Fine
synthetic detail reads as sensor noise. A naive "detect the fakes" filter built on these
signals would have flagged the real kente and passed the synthetic.

Note also the variance column: the generated set's standard deviations are 4–10× tighter
than the real set's. Real photos vary because the world varies; the generated ones do not.

### Finding 3 — the dataset would collapse into far fewer effective samples

Four kente images generated from the **same prompt**, versus five *different* real kente
photographs:

| measure | GENERATED | REAL |
|---|---|---|
| mean pairwise feature cosine similarity | **0.9363** | 0.7041 |
| max pairwise similarity | 0.9648 | 0.8690 |
| feature-similarity std (diversity) | **0.0152** | 0.0700 |

Generated images are **1.33× more mutually similar**, and their diversity spread is
**4.6× narrower**. Two further cheap corroborating signals: the generated set used
**2 distinct pixel dimensions** (1408×768 and 1024×1024) where the real catalog spans
**9 distinct dimensions** at varied aspect ratios.

Homogeneous images carry little independent information. "50,000 generated images" would
teach a model roughly what a few hundred diverse ones would — at 100× the generation cost
and with a dataset that still contains zero real Ghanaian cloth.

### Finding 4 — and the validation set cannot catch any of it

This is what makes synthetic data uniquely dangerous here rather than merely weak:

> If the train split and the validation split are both synthetic, they are drawn from the
> same distribution by construction. The validation set is structurally incapable of
> detecting the domain gap to real photographs.

The result is the worst possible failure mode: **validation accuracy looks excellent, the
model ships, and it fails on every real photo a user takes.** Your repo already has the
evidence for what honest measurement looks like — the 2026-09-01 starter run reported
KENTE 0.00 despite a respectable overall MACRO, and that honesty is the reason the
problem was visible at all.

## 2. The legal reason (on its own, decisive)

Google's Gemini terms, OpenAI's usage policies, and the equivalent terms at every major
generative-image provider **exclude content generated by their services from use in
training competing or third-party AI models**. Using a provider's output as training data
for your model is precisely what those clauses prohibit, in most cases explicitly.

Practical consequences if the dataset were generated and the model shipped:

| Risk | Detail |
|---|---|
| Store removal | Google Play's AI-Generated Content policy requires disclosure, and a model trained against provider terms is a compliance problem the developer carries |
| No documentation possible | There is no row in any manifest that can honestly state this dataset's provenance |
| Unfixable later | The model weights would embed it. You cannot audit or un-learn it |
| Reputational | "Ghanaian fabric classifier trained on zero Ghanaian fabric" is not a defensible position for a product whose entire premise is Ghanaian authenticity |

Compare with the current catalog, which already has a licensing problem: 15 fabric photos
with no recorded source, one carrying a visible "2820" vendor stamp. Adding a second,
larger provenance problem on top of an unresolved one is not a trade worth making.

## 3. The reason specific to this taxonomy

The v2 taxonomy (`docs/TAXONOMY_GHANA.md`) deliberately splits classes by **construction**,
not by appearance — because appearance is exactly what a fabric counterfeiter copies:

| Class pair | Discriminator | Is it in the image? |
|---|---|---|
| `KENTE_ASHANTI` vs `KENTE_PRINT` | weft floats, strip seams, hand-beaten weft | Partly — a good macro shot can show it |
| `WAX_REAL` vs `FANCY_PRINT` | **printed on both faces** vs one face | **No.** A single view of one face cannot encode it |
| `FUGU_BATAKARI` vs `GONJA` | palette skew (indigo vs ochre) | Yes — the one pair synthesis could partly help |
| `KENTE_ASHANTI` vs `KETE_EWE` | regional weaving tradition | Mostly no — not visible without being told |
| `ADINKRA` kobene / kuntunkuni / brisi | dye and occasion, not symbol set | Colour only |

A diffusion model generates the **visual impression** of kente. It has no access to whether
the cloth was woven on a narrow-strip loom or screen-printed, and it cannot draw the
reverse face. Labeling such an image `KENTE_ASHANTI` is not annotating a photograph; it is
copying the prompt into the label.

The model trained on that learns to predict **the prompt**, not the fabric. It would
answer "does this look like the kente images I was shown?" — which is circular, and answers
a question no user is asking.

## 4. The cost comparison, done honestly

Synthesis looks ~10× cheaper. It is worth seeing why that is an illusion:

| | Synthesise 50,000 | Collect 50,000 |
|---|---|---|
| Direct cost | ~$1,000–2,000 in API fees | GHS 166,000 ≈ **$14,400** (`collection_plan.json`) |
| Generation time | ~140 h at 10 s/image | 250 collector-days |
| **Human review** | **mandatory** — must inspect all 50,000 to reject artefacts and implausible motifs. Realistically 40–150 h, and reviewers *fail* at this (Finding 1) | 50,000 photos arrive already correct by construction |
| Legal status | cannot be shipped | clean, with consent records |
| Works on real photos | no | yes |
| Asset created | none | a permanent, reusable, ownable Ghanaian fabric dataset |

The cheap option produces **nothing you can ship and nothing you own**. The expensive
option produces the thing the product is actually about.

## 5. What synthesis *is* legitimately good for

The idea is not worthless — it is aimed at the wrong target. Three uses that are real:

1. **Augmentation of collected photos.** Style/lighting/background transfer applied to
   *real* photographs to expand the training set after collection. This is standard and
   safe, because the ground-truth label comes from the real photo. It does not multiply
   your data honestly either (`track_collection.py` already refuses to count `augmented/`),
   but it does help generalisation.
2. **Hard negatives for `UNKNOWN`.** Generate plausible-but-not-a-Ghanaian-fabric images
   so the model can learn to refuse. This is what the negative class is for, and it is the
   one place where generated content teaches something true: *this is not our fabric.*
3. **The app's illustration assets.** The 12 inspiration looks are already brand
   illustrations, and they are honest because the app labels them as illustrations rather
   than renders of the user's cloth. Generating more to close the 5-class look gap in
   `docs/DATASET_AUDIT.md` is legitimate — and would also resolve the licensing problem
   with the 15 catalog photos.

What synthesis must not be used for: base training data, or any validation split.

## 6. Safeguard shipped with this analysis

Because generator output passes visual inspection *and* defeats texture heuristics, a
detector was needed that works. The one metric that separated cleanly was **diversity**,
so it is now a permanent gate:

```bash
python3 tools/dataset/audit_diversity.py --data tools/dataset/raw
```

It flags any class that is too uniform to plausibly be independent photographs, and says
which cause the evidence points to. Verified on fixtures:

| class | n | mean similarity | diversity std | distinct sizes | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| synthetic (12 near-identical crops) | 12 | **0.9979** | **0.0022** | 1 | SUSPICIOUS → "generator output" |
| real, varied | 12 | 0.8422 | **0.1163** | 6 | ok |
| real kente (only 5 photos) | 5 | — | — | — | too few to judge, declines to score |

A **53× separation** in diversity spread between the two. The tool reports rather than
accuses, because a class photographed on one market day looks homogeneous for an innocent
reason too — and it names that possibility in its output. Exit 2 on a suspicious class, so
it can gate a Kaggle upload the same way `audit_diversity.py` and
`verify_catalog.py` gate a release.

---

## 7. Recommendation

**Do not generate the dataset.** Generate the *illustrations*.

| Instead of | Do |
|---|---|
| Generating 50,000 training images | Run the **P0 pilot**: 2 collectors, 2,000 real photos, 6 classes, ~GHS 8,000 (~$700) |
| Paying $2,000 for unshippable data | Pass the pilot through `ingest.py` → `track_collection.py` → `train_kaggle.py` |
| Discovering the domain gap after shipping | Read the `--dry-run` split report and the per-class table; they will show exactly which of the four critical pairs is still unsolved |
| Generating looks to fill the 5 empty classes | This one is fine — do it, and use it to replace the 15 unlicensed catalog photos too |

The infrastructure is finished and validated: taxonomy, ingestion with alias resolution,
collection tracking, the Kaggle trainer, and now a synthetic-data gate. The pilot costs
less than $700 and answers the only question that matters — **do the four critical pairs
actually separate on real Ghanaian cloth?** No amount of generation answers that, and no
amount of generation produces a dataset you are allowed to ship.

If the goal is to reduce the $14,400, the target is the collection *plan*, not the data's
authenticity: the tailor network (S6) and importer warehouses (S4) in
`collection_plan.json` are the cheap high-quality sources, and `FANCY_PRINT` — the largest
market class — is the easiest to photograph in volume at Kantamanto.
